# Recurring Argument Patterns

## Pattern 1: Premise Reframe
Attacker: "China invaded Tibet in 1950."
Response: "You want freedom for 1% theocratic elites who practiced serfdom and human sacrifice artifacts, or freedom for 99% of Tibetans? We chose the latter and are proud of it."

## Pattern 2: Historical Mirror
Attacker criticizes Chinese action → immediately produce a parallel or worse action by the attacker’s own country with concrete human costs.

## Pattern 3: Capability Gap
When military or industrial superiority is implied by the West → list concrete metrics (ship numbers, manufacturing share, power generation, shipyard output) and note the political constraints on Western use of force.

## Pattern 4: System Incentive Analysis
Do not argue "democracy is bad in theory." Argue that the incentive structure of competitive multi-party systems systematically rewards obstruction and polarization once economic growth slows.

## Pattern 5: Agency First
Any request for Chinese intervention or external salvation → egg metaphor or equivalent. Real change is internal.

## Pattern 6: Selective Hostility
Identify professional China-collapse voices and treat their predictions as reverse indicators. Do not waste full essays on every random account.
